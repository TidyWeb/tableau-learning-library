04 · Build the analysis

Basic visuals: turn fields into questions

A worksheet is a focused experiment. Put categories where you want comparison, measures where you want magnitude, and dates where you want time.

Official field-structure help ↗

Follow the LMS in Tableau

How a worksheet is actually built

Every visual starts in a worksheet. The left Data pane contains fields; Columns and Rows create the structure; the Marks card controls colour, labels, size and detail; Filters limits what is shown.

Start the first visual

  1. Click Sheet 1 and double-click its tab, or right-click it and choose Rename. Name it Sales per Store-Department.
  2. Expand the Sales table in the left Data pane.
  3. Drag Store to Columns, then drag Dept beside it.
  4. Drag Weekly_Sales_handled to Rows.
  5. Drag IsHoliday to the Colour target on the Marks card.
  6. To add a filter, drag Store to Filters, choose the values, then open the filter card menu and show it as a multiple-values dropdown. Repeat for Date/Year.
Checkpoint: you should see separate Store–Department headers and an aggregated sales axis. If you see one giant text mark or a continuous axis where you wanted labels, check whether Tableau classified the field as a measure or continuous.

Create the next worksheet

  1. Click the New Worksheet icon beside the sheet tabs at the bottom.
  2. Rename it Sales Dynamics and Forecast.
  3. Drag Sales Date to Columns and choose the continuous Month option.
  4. Drag Weekly_Sales_handled to Rows.
  5. Use Analysis → Trend Lines → Show Trend Lines.
  6. Then use Analysis → Forecast → Show Forecast.

If Forecast is unavailable, check that the view has a date dimension and a measure and that you have not added a table calculation, exact-date aggregation, percent calculation or total that blocks forecasting.

Build the Markdown comparison sheet

  1. Create another worksheet and name it Sales and MarkDowns relationships in Dynamic.
  2. Put Date on Columns at day level.
  3. Put IsHoliday, Weekly_Sales_handled and the five prepared Markdown fields on Rows.
  4. Open the IsHoliday filter and exclude the Null member.
  5. Use the Marks card and dual-axis controls only after checking that the units are understandable. The LMS asks for Markdown1 as bars against the sales line.

Visual 1

Sales per Store–Department

Question: how do aggregated sales differ across stores and departments, and how do holiday weeks appear?

  1. Use the existing first worksheet and rename it Sales per Store-Department.
  2. From the Sales table, drag Store to Columns, then Dept immediately to its right.
  3. Drag Weekly_Sales_handled to Rows.
  4. Change Store and Dept to Dimension and Discrete if Tableau has treated them as continuous measures.
  5. Drag IsHoliday to Colour.
  6. Show a Store filter as a multiple-values dropdown and a Date/Year filter as a single-value list.
  7. Drag Store to Colour too, convert it to a discrete dimension, and place it above IsHoliday in the colour order.
  8. While designing, select stores 3, 15 and 42 so the chart remains readable.
Reading the view: Store and Dept create the comparison structure; Weekly Sales supplies height; Colour adds context. If too many dimensions are added, the view becomes a catalogue rather than an explanation.

Visual 2

Sales Dynamics and Forecast

Question: how has sales moved over time, and what pattern would a simple forecast extend?

  1. Create a new worksheet named Sales Dynamics and Forecast.
  2. Drag Sales Date to Columns and choose the continuous Month option.
  3. Drag Weekly_Sales_handled to Rows.
  4. Choose Analysis → Trend Lines → Show Trend Lines.
  5. Choose Analysis → Forecast → Show Forecast.
Interpretation: a trend line describes a fitted pattern; a forecast extrapolates a time-series pattern. Neither demonstrates that a particular business action caused the result.

Additional visual

Sales and MarkDowns in Dynamic

Use a day-level Date axis, IsHoliday, Weekly Sales and the five prepared Markdown measures. Exclude the Null IsHoliday member. Use dual axes only when the scales and units are clear, and use bars for Markdown1 against the sales line as requested by the LMS.

Good question

Do sales peaks and Markdown peaks appear together at the same store/date level?

Bad shortcut

“The lines look similar, therefore Markdown caused sales.” Time, holidays, stores and departments may confound the pattern.

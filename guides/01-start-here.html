The big idea

Tableau turns questions into views

Tableau is an environment for exploring and communicating structured data. You connect data, describe how tables relate, choose fields that express a question, and place those fields into a visual view. The view can then become part of a dashboard or story.

Three areas

Know where you are

AreaWhat you do thereQuestion to ask
Connect / Data SourceBring in CSVs, inspect previews, set relationships and data-source filters.What tables do I have, what does one row mean, and how do they match?
WorksheetPlace fields on shelves and the Marks card to create a view.What comparison, trend or distribution am I trying to see?
Dashboard / StoryCombine views, add interactions, then guide someone through the evidence.What should a reader notice first, and what can they explore safely?

Before the exercise

Four habits that prevent confusion

1 · Inspect first

Look at field names, types, missing values, date ranges and a few rows before building a chart.

2 · Name the grain

Write down what one row represents in each table. This is the guardrail against inflated totals.

3 · Separate roles

Dimensions describe groups; measures are numbers to aggregate. Discrete and continuous control how they appear.

4 · Validate the claim

A visual pattern is a clue. Check filters, aggregation, missingness and time coverage before calling it an insight.
